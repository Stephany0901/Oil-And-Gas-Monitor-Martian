# Oil & Gas Briefing — Wednesday, October 28, 2026

## Market
Crude pulled back modestly on Tuesday but remains near six-month highs. Brent settled at $113.88 (-$1.93, -1.7%) and WTI at $99.90 (-$1.86, -1.8%) as diplomatic talks involving Iran, Saudi Arabia, Turkey and Pakistan eased some immediate supply fears. Brent topped $117 last week, its highest since April, after Iranian attacks on tankers near the Strait of Hormuz. Attacks on Hormuz shipping hit a record in October, a Houthi missile strike on Saudi Arabia's Ras Tanura refinery was claimed, and there is still no deal to end the US-Israeli war with Iran. Israel's election adds uncertainty; SEB says the market carries a significant risk premium. UBS lifted its year-end Brent forecast to about $110 from $100. Russian Baltic/Black Sea crude exports are expected to fall in November.

Natural gas is the laggard. November Henry Hub futures rose about 2% to $2.662/MMBtu on Tuesday, a bounce on short-covering ahead of expiry; December trades near $3.02. Next-day Henry Hub spot fell to $2.52, its lowest since November 2024. Mild weather through Nov. 11 limits heating demand, though LNG feedgas is running about 19.2 Bcfd and Lower 48 output is about 113.5 Bcfd. A 68 Bcf injection was expected for the week ended Oct. 23, taking storage to roughly 3,712 Bcf (about 0.7% above the five-year average). Global prices (TTF ~$31, JKM ~$32) are far above U.S. levels, but US export capacity is already near full.

OPEC+: the seven-country group agreed on Oct. 4 to hold November targets unchanged (Saudi 10.5 mb/d, Russia 9.9). Actual output is below target because of Hormuz export disruptions and constraints on Russian flows. Next meeting is reportedly November 1. Macro: the Fed is widely expected to hold, and CPI is due Oct. 29, so a hot print with crude near $100 could keep energy in focus.
