# Oil & Gas Briefing — Monday, November 23, 2026

*Pre-open edition: US equities have not traded since Friday, Nov 20, so price and mover data still reflect that session. Weekend news is included below. Educational only, not investment advice.*

## Market

Brent (Jan) settled at **$92.57** on Friday. That was up about 0.6% on the day but down about **3.4% on the week**, its third straight weekly loss. WTI (Jan) closed around **$83.4–84.0**. The weekend added fresh risk to Monday's open, though. On Saturday, UKMTO reported a tanker hit about 11 nm north of Khasab, Oman, with a possible second hit nearby. Iran said the ship struck a naval mine; Reuters could not verify that. The Houthis also told workers to leave Saudi Red Sea ports after Saudi-led strikes on Hodeidah. Separately, Japan agreed to buy up to 8 mb of Saudi crude for its reserve and to lend Saudi Arabia 16 mb of storage in Japan. That gives Saudi barrels a home outside the Gulf. Pulling the other way is the supply-recovery story. The IEA sees a Q4 surplus of about 1.7 mb/d, Middle East exports are near pre-war levels, and the EIA STEO expects inventories to build about 3.5 mb/d in H1 2027. Expect a headline-driven open, with the sellers still in control of the trend.

In natural gas, Henry Hub rose about **5.6% on the week to ~$3.41**, close to its 11-month high. Drivers are record LNG feedgas and a colder outlook. In Europe, TTF jumped 4.7% on Friday to €88.45. A polar-vortex breakdown threatens storage that is only ~70% full, about 12 points below last year. LNG exits from Hormuz have also thinned to about two tankers a week, down from eight. This is a tailwind for US LNG exporters (LNG) and gas-weighted E&Ps (EQT, AR, RRC).

OPEC+: the **Sunday, Nov 29** ministerial meeting sets 2027 quotas under the new capacity-based baseline. It is the week's key catalyst. Holiday note: US markets are **closed Thu Nov 26** for Thanksgiving and close early Fri Nov 27, so liquidity will be thin.
