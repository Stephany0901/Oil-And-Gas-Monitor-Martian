## Market

**Crude.** Brent was about $103.4/bbl on Friday morning, down roughly $0.40 on the day. It is up about 6% over the past month and about 57% year over year. WTI levels were not confirmed in sources I could open. Headlines this week point to a choppy tape: Hormuz flows are recovering, but US–Iran talks have stalled, and the Iran/Hormuz disruption remains the dominant driver. OECD inventories were reported about 66 million barrels below the five-year average as of June.

**OPEC+.** The group met today (Oct 4) and, per multiple wire headlines (CNBC, Reuters, UPI, The National), agreed to keep November output targets unchanged. Analysts have noted that with Hormuz constrained, quotas have limited influence on physical supply. I could not open the full articles, so treat details as headline-level.

**Natural gas.** Henry Hub futures settled at $3.03/MMBtu on Sep 30. Storage was 3,415 Bcf for the week ending Sep 25 (+64 Bcf injection), and the EIA expects end-October inventories near 4 Tcf, the second-highest on record. Production is at record levels, about 4% above 2025. Demand is up 5% year over year, and LNG feedgas averaged 18.0 Bcf/d in September. Supply is ample, which caps price upside.

**Companies.** No major M&A headline turned up. Chevron and Exxon report Q3 on Oct 30; consensus EPS is about $4.32 for CVX and $3.40 for XOM. Themes are Chevron's Venezuela expansion and Hess integration, and Exxon's LNG projects.
