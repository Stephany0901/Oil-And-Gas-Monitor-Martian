# Oil & Gas Equity Monitor — Monday, August 17, 2026

*Educational market monitoring, not investment advice. Prices are last completed session (Friday, Aug 14) closes.*

## Market

Crude closed last week firmly bid on renewed Iran escalation. Brent settled at $88.52 (+1.67% on Friday) and WTI at $82.40 (+1.42%), with both benchmarks up more than 5% on the week. The driver is policy, not fundamentals: Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said Washington would impose measures aimed at the "economic isolation" of Iran that "have never been seen," while maintaining the naval blockade of Iranian ports. Iran's Foreign Ministry has restated that Hormuz will not fully reopen while the blockade stands, and Iran–Oman talks have not produced an agreement. The Strait has been disrupted for most of the past five months; SPR releases and bypass pipelines have kept the market from pricing a full closure, which is why crude sits well below its crisis highs even as a near-term deal looks less likely.

Natural gas is the opposite story. Henry Hub was around $2.74/MMBtu on Aug 13, down from $3.22 at the start of July, pressured by record production, ample storage, and softer LNG feedgas demand. EIA's larger-than-expected injection added to the drag. The full-year 2026 average forecast remains near $3.31, implying the strip expects a second-half recovery that has not yet arrived.

OPEC+ approved a 188 kb/d quota increase for September, completing the unwind of the 2023 voluntary cuts. That removes a recurring monthly catalyst and shifts attention back to spare capacity and Hormuz risk.

The equity story remains refining. Global capacity losses — roughly 4.5 mb/d of Q2 closures and war damage — pushed the US 3-2-1 crack spread to about $59/bbl, a record. MPC, VLO, and PSX are all 40–55% above their 200-day averages, and no name on the watchlist trades below its 200-day.
