# Oil & Gas Briefing — Tuesday, November 17, 2026

## Market

Crude moved up and down within a narrow range on Tuesday. Two forces pulled against each other. On one side were tanker attacks around the Strait of Hormuz and Iran's third strike in under a week on a U.S. radar site in Bahrain; a third U.S. carrier group reached the Gulf on Monday. On the other side were strong Gulf export flows: Hormuz flows rose 78% in Q3 to about 9.1 mb/d, according to the EIA. Brent traded around $92.5–93.5 and WTI around $85.5–86.5. That followed a 3.6% drop in Brent on Monday, which left it near its lowest level since August and well below the ~$118 spike in late October. Brent's prompt backwardation has narrowed to about $2.4 from more than $3 a month ago. The IEA sees a surplus of roughly 2 mb/d this quarter, and Goldman is flagging glut risk for 2027. Saudi Arabia and the UAE have cut their official selling prices, and Iraq restarted the 120 kb/d Kirkuk–Fishkhabour line to Turkey.

**Natural gas:** December Henry Hub bounced 2.8% to settle at $3.208/MMBtu, after touching its lowest level since Nov 9; short covering drove much of the move. The bounce looks fragile. The two-week heating-degree-day forecast fell to 216 from 275, NOAA expects above-normal temperatures in late November, storage is about 2.4% above the 5-year average, and the EIA's November STEO cut its Nov–Mar Henry Hub forecast 16% to $2.87.

**OPEC+:** In early November the core group held December quotas flat. It has started an audit of member capacity (DeGolyer & MacNaughton) ahead of the **Nov 29 full ministerial meeting**, which will set 2027 policy. That meeting is the next big catalyst.

**Company headlines:** Diamondback (FANG) signed a deal to buy $2.15B of Permian assets from Double Eagle. CNQ agreed to buy Advantage Energy for $1.77B. Chevron is reportedly close to a ~$5B deal for Beacon Offshore in the Gulf of Mexico (not confirmed). Exxon started production at Uaru in Guyana, lifting capacity there to about 1.1 mb/d. Shale hedge books are a drag: about $1.3B of realized Q3 losses, with Devon the largest at $262M. Equities slipped across the sector: XLE −0.5%, XOP −1.3%, XES −1.9%.
