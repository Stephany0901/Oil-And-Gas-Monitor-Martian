# Oil & Gas Equity Monitor — Wednesday, July 29, 2026

*Educational market monitoring, not investment advice. Prices reflect the last completed session (July 28, 2026) unless noted.*

## Market

Crude is unwinding its war premium at speed. Brent traded around $86.58/bbl and WTI around $79.28/bbl on July 28 — a third consecutive losing session and the lowest level in more than a week. WTI was down roughly 4% on the day, following Monday's roughly 8–9% collapse, the largest single-day drop in over three months. The driver is diplomatic rather than physical: President Trump described talks with Tehran as "good" and signalled a "good chance" of resolution after pausing military strikes, which has traders pricing Iranian barrels and normalized Hormuz transit back into supply. Context matters for scale — Brent is still up roughly 25% month-to-date, so this is premium compression, not a demand scare.

Risk has not disappeared. Saudi Arabia intercepted drones aimed at oil facilities, Houthi forces claimed an attack on the East-West pipeline, and Saudi Aramco reportedly shut its Jazan refinery after an attack. Disruption has spread from Hormuz to the Red Sea, the main alternative export route for Saudi crude.

On supply, OPEC+ core members approved a 188,000 bbl/d increase for August at their July 5 meeting — the fifth consecutive monthly hike as 2023 voluntary cuts unwind, led by 62,000 bbl/d each from Saudi Arabia and Russia, with Iraq, Kuwait, Kazakhstan, Algeria and Oman contributing the balance.

Natural gas is a separate and weaker story. Henry Hub fell to roughly $2.72/MMBtu, an 11-week low, on record Lower 48 output averaging 110.6 bcfd in July versus 110.0 bcfd in June, comfortable storage, and soft LNG feedgas demand. Cheniere did receive approval to introduce feedgas at its final expansion train — a modest structural offset, but not enough to change the near-term balance.

## Screen idea

Screening for a below-200-day price combined with a low TTM EV/EBITDA leaves exactly one candidate: **Halliburton (HAL)** — the sole watchlist name under its 200-day (-7.1%), at **7.8x EV/EBITDA**, 1.5x net debt/EBITDA and a 6.6% free cash flow yield. It is not the cheapest on the multiple, though: that is **Devon (DVN) at 4.7x**, then **OXY at 5.7x** and **COP at 6.4x**, all three trading above their 200-day averages (DVN only marginally, +2.4%). SLB is the most expensive of the five screened at 11.7x.

The honest read is that the value-plus-weakness overlap is thin, because the sector spent July rallying on a supply shock that is now reversing. The more useful framing is the split: services (HAL, SLB, BKR, XES) hold the technical damage, while E&P trailing multiples look cheap largely because they capitalize a quarter of spike-elevated realizations — trailing EV/EBITDA flatters any producer after a price spike. DVN at 4.7x and OXY at 5.7x are worth examining on forward rather than trailing numbers, particularly ahead of their August 4 and August 5 reports.
