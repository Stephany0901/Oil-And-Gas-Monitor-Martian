# Oil & Gas Equity Monitor — Tuesday, September 22, 2026

## Market

Crude gave back another chunk of its supply-shock premium. Brent settled near $100.06/bbl on Monday, down about 3.7% on the day, with WTI around $97.56, off roughly 2.7% — both now well below the ~$108-110 spike printed on September 14. The driver is repair progress on Saudi Arabia's 1,200-km East-West pipeline, shut after the September 11 drone strikes that damaged three pumping stations. Reuters-sourced reporting put full repairs at five to six weeks, but engineers are said to be able to bring roughly 2.0-2.5 million bpd of the line's ~7 million bpd capacity back sooner. That reframes the event from a structural loss of ~4% of global supply to a timing problem, and the market has been repricing accordingly for a week.

The underlying situation is still tight, which is why $100 is the floor of the retreat rather than $80. The East-West line matters because it is Saudi Arabia's bypass around the Hormuz disruption, letting barrels load from the Red Sea instead. Saudi crude output was reported to OPEC at 6.2 million bpd in August, versus 10.9 million bpd in February. OPEC+ approved a 188,000 bpd hike for September, completing the rollback of the 2023 voluntary cuts, and has signaled quotas hold steady through year-end — so there is little incremental policy supply on the way.

Natural gas is trading its own weather-driven story, with Henry Hub around $2.85-2.91/MMBtu. The EIA's below-average storage build for the week ended September 11 cut the surplus to the five-year average to 118 Bcf from 148 Bcf, but cooler forecasts for September 22 to October 1 across the South and Southeast trimmed late-season power burn expectations. LNG feedgas is the bullish offset: flows to the nine major US export terminals have averaged 18.3 bcfd in September versus 17.2 bcfd in August.
