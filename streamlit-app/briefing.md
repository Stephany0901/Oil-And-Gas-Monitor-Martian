## Market

The new item this weekend is Hormuz headline risk. Iran's IRGC Navy said two tankers hit mines late Friday, and Iran's regular navy said a third struck a mine east of the strait on Saturday morning. Neither claim named a ship. The US military disputes both claims and says the main shipping routes are clear. Unless the damage is confirmed, the setup looks like last Thursday's: an unconfirmed Houthi strike claim on Saudi sites sparked a 3.3% pop, which faded within a day. Brent settled at $95.84 on Friday (flat on the day, about −2.5% on the week, ~20% off its late-October high near $118). WTI settled at $88.56. Commonly cited levels for tonight's open are $98, the Nov 6 settle area and the level confirmed damage could push Brent toward, and $91, near the Nov 10 low settle of $91.49. The bigger trend hasn't changed. A supply surplus is building because OPEC+ output rose in October, led by Saudi Arabia, and US crude and product stocks rose. A December Fed hike is now priced in after the hot PPI print, and the US blockade of Iranian barrels is still in place.

Natural gas: December Henry Hub ended Friday near $3.23 (−3% on the day, still about +8% on the week). Warmer forecasts are offsetting record LNG feedgas of more than 20 Bcf/d. Storage stands at 3,862 Bcf, above the five-year average, and output is near 112 Bcf/d. Funds hold a record net short, which leaves gas prone to short-covering spikes on any cold turn.

OPEC+: December targets are unchanged and quota hikes stay paused through year-end. Gulf producers are still below quota because of Hormuz constraints. The next big date is the Nov 29 full ministerial meeting, which sets 2027 policy and decides whether to restore the halted 2022 layer.
