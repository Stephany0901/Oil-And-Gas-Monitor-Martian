# Oil & Gas Equity Monitor — Thursday, July 30, 2026

*Educational market summary. Not investment advice. Prices reflect the last completed session (July 29, 2026).*

## Market

Crude ripped higher on July 29 as the US–Iran conflict re-escalated after a brief diplomatic pause. Iran's Revolutionary Guard fired ballistic missiles at a US airbase and command center in the Middle East; President Trump responded that the US "will be hitting them hard." Brent settled +7.9% at $90.74 and WTI +6.6% at $84.46, putting WTI's one-month gain near 22%. An early EIA read showing a 7.2 million-barrel US crude stock draw added fuel. The move reversed roughly half of a brutal three-session slide — Brent had shed about 16% into July 28, its worst such stretch since 2020, on hopes a ceasefire framework would hold. Context for how wide the range has been: Brent traded above $100 intraday on July 24 and closed at $84.09 on July 28.

The supply story behind the volatility is physical, not sentiment. With Hormuz effectively shut, Saudi Arabia has been routing crude through the East-West (Petroline) pipeline to Yanbu, which handled roughly 92% of Saudi seaborne exports in June. Houthi drone strikes have targeted exactly that chokepoint — the Jizan refinery on July 25, then the Abqaiq stabilization plant and an East-West pumping station on July 27 — cutting Saudi production capacity by an estimated ~600 kb/d and pipeline throughput by ~700 kb/d. OPEC+ spare capacity, nominally ~3 mb/d and concentrated in Saudi Arabia, is worth less when the export route is the binding constraint. The group's August quota step-up was a token 188 kb/d across seven members.

Natural gas is trading on its own fundamentals and going nowhere. The front-month closed 2.722, up 2.3% on the day but below its 10-day average of 2.836 and down from 2.925 a week earlier. Strong year-over-year production growth and comfortable storage are capping rallies despite above-normal temperatures and record LNG feedgas demand.

Equities lagged the commodity, as they usually do on geopolitical spikes. XLE +1.9%, XOP +3.4%, while the S&P 500 fell 0.7% and the Dow dropped roughly 760 points; the Fed held rates steady. Refining and midstream barely moved — cracks are already at four-year highs and those groups have run hard.

## Screen idea

**Below the 200-day average, cheap on EV/EBITDA.** Of the 22 symbols priced, exactly one trades below its 200-day SMA: **HAL** at $31.19 versus a 200-day of $33.64 (–7.3%), at 7.8x TTM EV/EBITDA with 1.5x net debt/EBITDA and a 6.6% free cash flow yield. It is the only name clearing the technical filter, so it wins by default rather than on merit — 7.8x is mid-pack, not cheap.

Worth stating plainly: **HAL is not the cheapest name on EV/EBITDA. DVN is, at 4.9x** (0.96x net debt/EBITDA, 9.5% FCF yield), followed by OXY at 5.9x and EOG at 6.7x — but all three sit above their 200-day averages (+6.6%, +11.9%, +18.1% respectively), DVN by the smallest margin. SLB screens most expensive at 11.5x. The honest read on a sector that has rallied this hard on geopolitics is that "cheap and below trend" barely exists right now: DVN at 4.9x and only 6.6% above trend is the closest thing to both, and HAL is the only genuine technical laggard. Whether that laggard status reflects a structural North American completions problem or an overshoot is a question the August 4 WTTR and August 5 HP prints will help answer.
