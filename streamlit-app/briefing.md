# September 23, 2026

## Market

Crude sold off into the close: WTI settled near $92.16 (-2.6% on the day, its fourth straight down session and roughly 8% off last Friday's ~$100 close), while Brent held firmer at $102.86 (-0.2%), leaving an unusually wide ~$10.70 Brent-WTI spread. The move traces to reports that Iran told mediators it could reopen the Strait of Hormuz within seven days if Washington eases military pressure and lifts its naval blockade — a fragile but real off-ramp from the standoff that has underpinned crude's geopolitical premium through 2026. Saudi Arabia has already restarted its East-West bypass pipeline (~5 million bpd capacity) as a workaround, and Trump's UN remarks were read as tempering near-term escalation risk, pushing WTI to two-week lows. Brent, more directly tied to Gulf-transit barrels, stayed better supported.

The bigger structural story right now is downstream, not upstream. Ukrainian drone strikes have pushed Russian refining throughput to a 24-year low (~3.9 million bpd), and Moscow's gasoline/diesel/jet-fuel export bans have starved global distillate markets. US diesel prices are at records; Trump is reportedly weighing a US diesel export ban, and Maine's two senators asked him this week to release SPR heating-oil reserves ahead of winter. That backdrop pressured refiners (MPC, VLO both lower today) even as upstream producers rallied.

Natural gas extended its recent climb, closing at $3.02/MMBtu (+2.0%), up about 6.6% over the last three sessions on firmer power-sector and LNG demand.

OPEC+ completed its rollback of pandemic-era voluntary cuts with the September quota, and delegates say the group plans to pause further increases after this month — leaving little near-term OPEC+ supply catalyst in either direction.

Separately, the 10-year Treasury yield spiked to 5.14% (a 19-year high) and equities broadly sold off (Dow -0.68%) — a macro risk-off backdrop that energy names mostly shrugged off today.
