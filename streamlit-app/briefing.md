# Oil & Gas Briefing — Wednesday, September 23, 2026

## Market

Crude extended its losing streak to five sessions on Tuesday (Sep 22). WTI fell about 3% to roughly $89.60, its first close below $90 in over two weeks. Brent dropped about 1.7% to around $98.40, after trading above $101 on Monday morning. The driver is diplomacy. In his UN address, President Trump said US officials had a "very good meeting" with Iranian envoys and that he expects a deal after the November midterms. Kyodo reported that Tehran has offered to reopen the Strait of Hormuz within seven days if the US lifts its blockade of Iranian ports. Physical flows are also easing: Saudi Arabia has been moving about 2.9 mb/d through Hormuz, supertankers are stacking up at its Gulf terminals, and it is preparing to restart the East-West pipeline (shut after drone strikes) with meaningful flows targeted by this weekend. The risk premium is fragile. Trump also floated "annihilating" Iran as the alternative, so headline whipsaw remains likely. API showed a 1.8M bbl crude build; EIA inventory data is due today.

Natural gas moved against crude. Front-month Henry Hub jumped about 6.7% to about $3.03/MMBtu, a 10-week high. Lower 48 output fell to its lowest since early August, and forecasts show the season's first real heating demand. EU gas slipped about 2.6%.

OPEC+ has no fresh news this week. The core eight kept October targets unchanged on Sep 6 and meet again on **Oct 4** to set baselines and decide how to unwind the remaining cuts. Saudi August output collapsed to about 6.24 mb/d, its lowest since 1990, because of Houthi attacks on Red Sea infrastructure.

Equities: refiners led the sector lower as crude fell. Diesel cracks are still near records (US diesel crack hit an all-time high of about $102/bbl this month), but the trade is crowded after six straight weekly gains. No major sector M&A was announced in the last 24h.
