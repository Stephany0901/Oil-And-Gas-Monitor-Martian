# Oil & Gas Equity Monitor — Tuesday, August 18, 2026

*Educational market summary, not investment advice. Prices reflect the 17 Aug close, the last completed session.*

## Market

Crude remains the dominant variable and the dominant risk. WTI settled around $84.95/bbl on 17 August, up roughly 3.1% on the day, while Brent finished near $88.31, essentially flat to marginally lower (-0.2%). That divergence is unusual and most likely a settlement-timing artifact between the two benchmark series rather than a real narrowing of the transatlantic spread — treat the roughly $3.4 gap as approximate until both marks confirm on the same clock.

The macro driver is unchanged: the Strait of Hormuz has been effectively closed since late February, and the Washington–Tehran talks to reopen it have not produced an agreement. The IEA now expects 2026 global oil demand to fall by about 1.6 mb/d as high pump prices and shipping disruption bite, while global supply in July ran roughly 6.3 mb/d below year-ago levels. That is a market clearing its imbalance through inventory rather than production, and the agency has flagged the pace of stockdraw as the pressing concern. Brent has traded a violent range this year — above $100 at one point, near $70 at another — which is the single best argument for sizing energy exposure conservatively.

On supply policy, seven OPEC+ members agreed on 5 July to add 188 kb/d for August, matching the June increment. The group has been explicit that the taper of voluntary cuts can be paused or reversed. Notably, the UAE exited the group in May, which is why the headline increments have been revised smaller.

Natural gas is the mirror image. Henry Hub spot sits near $2.81/MMBtu after futures fell roughly 15% through July (from $3.22 to $2.75), pressured by record production, softer LNG feedgas demand and inventories well above the five-year average. Forecasters have diverged: the EIA models a $3.67 2026 average, the AGA a more bearish $3.31.
