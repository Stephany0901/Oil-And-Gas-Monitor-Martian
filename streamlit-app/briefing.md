## Market

Crude has pulled back hard from its war-driven highs. Brent settled around $90.95/bbl on Jul 26, down roughly 7.6% on the day, with WTI in the low-$90s after opening near $92.39 on Jul 24. The dominant driver is an easing of the Middle East risk premium: traffic through the Strait of Hormuz has been normalizing as peace negotiations progress, pulling barrels back to market even as sporadic US strikes on Iranian military and maritime targets continued. Traders are also positioning into a heavy macro week — API and EIA inventory data, US Q2 GDP, and a Federal Reserve rate decision — which is keeping intraday volatility elevated. Despite the retreat, prices remain high enough that integrated majors are set for a bumper Q2, a reminder that the tape is normalizing from an elevated base rather than collapsing.

Natural gas is firmer but rangebound. Henry Hub sits near $2.88–2.95/MMBtu, rebounding off a two-month low as forecasts for hotter-than-normal weather through Aug 7 lift power-burn and cooling demand. The offset is comfortable storage: inventories remain about 6.4% above the five-year seasonal average, capping upside.

On supply, seven OPEC+ producers (Saudi Arabia, Russia, Iraq, Kuwait, Kazakhstan, Algeria, Oman) agreed on Jul 5 to trim collective output by ~188,000 b/d beginning in August, citing market stability, with the right to pause or reverse. The group's next review is scheduled for Aug 2 — a near-term catalyst worth watching. Company-level news is dominated by strong oilfield-services earnings (see below).

## Screen idea

Screening the watchlist for names trading *below* their 200-day moving average and cheap on EV/EBITDA (TTM), only two candidates were actually below trend: **FANG** (~6% below 200-day, but richer at ~13.2x EV/EBITDA) and **HAL** (~0.5% below 200-day at ~8.2x EV/EBITDA). The name best combining the two screens is **Halliburton (HAL)** — it is both below its 200-day and carries the lowest EV/EBITDA of the sub-trend group, offering a value-plus-mean-reversion setup with optionality on a North America activity recovery (caveat: HAL just rallied ~2% on the services earnings wave and may not stay below its 200-day). For context, **DVN** is the outright cheapest watchlist name on EV/EBITDA (~4.9x) but trades *above* its 200-day, so it fails the below-trend leg of the screen.
