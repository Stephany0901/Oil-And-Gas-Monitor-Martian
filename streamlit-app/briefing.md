# Oil & Gas Briefing — Tuesday, November 24, 2026

*Pre-open edition. Price and mover data reflect Monday's (Nov 23) US session. Educational only, not investment advice.*

## Market

Crude broke lower on Monday. Weekend attack headlines did not stop the slide: a tanker was hit off Khasab, Oman, and the Houthis claimed a strike on a Saudi refinery (unconfirmed). **Brent (Jan) settled at $89.37, down 3.5%** from Friday's $92.57. It touched about $89.07 intraday, its lowest since early September. **WTI (Jan) traded near $80.1–80.5, down roughly 3.5–4%**, its lowest since late August. The market traded the surplus rather than the risk. Drivers included the IEA's ~1.7 mb/d Q4 surplus, the restart of Libya's Sharara pipeline and Middle East exports near pre-war levels. Physical markets are also easing. Goldman notes Atlantic-basin crude freight is down 60–70% from its October peak, and Dated Brent's premium has collapsed from about $31 in early October to about $1. JPMorgan's high-$70s view for 1H27 and fading odds of new US strikes on Iran added pressure. This is now a fourth leg down after three straight weekly losses.

**Natural gas** gave back Friday's 4.4% pop. December Henry Hub slid about 3% to roughly **$3.12/MMBtu** as weather models dropped heating degree days heading into Thanksgiving week. December expiry and an SNG force majeure after a pipeline explosion added noise. Record LNG feedgas still supports the winter picture. The European tightness (TTF near €88, storage about 70% full) remains a tailwind for US exporters.

**OPEC+:** the **Sunday, Nov 29** ministerial meeting will set 2027 quotas under the new capacity-based baseline. December targets were held flat on Nov 1. With Brent back below $90, the market will watch for any signal of restraint. **Holiday:** US markets are closed Thursday Nov 26 and close early Friday Nov 27. The EIA weekly inventory report moves to Wednesday.
