# Oil & Gas Equity Monitor — Tuesday, July 28, 2026

## Market

Crude gave back a large chunk of the war premium on Monday. WTI settled near $82.62/bbl, down roughly 8.7% on the day, and Brent fell to about $90.28/bbl, off 8.2%, after the US and Iran halted strikes over the weekend and diplomatic channels reopened. That was the sharpest single-day decline of the conflict to date, and it came without any physical change on the water — traffic through the Strait of Hormuz remains close to a standstill, with Iran still requiring vessels to coordinate transits through its Persian Gulf Strait Authority. The market is pricing the probability of a durable de-escalation rather than the current barrel count, which is why the tape has been violent in both directions. Even after Monday's slide, crude is up more than 20% month-to-date.

On supply, the seven core OPEC+ producers agreed in early July to lift August quotas by another 188 kb/d, the fifth consecutive monthly increase and part of the phased unwind of the 1.65 mb/d 2023 cut. Quota increases from April through July total nearly 800 kb/d, but much of that has stayed on paper because the Hormuz closure has stranded barrels from Saudi Arabia, Kuwait and Iraq. OPEC spare capacity sits near 6.8 mb/d. If transits normalize, that combination — restored exports plus five months of quota increases — is the bear case for crude into the autumn.

Natural gas is the mirror image: no geopolitical premium, plenty of supply. Henry Hub prompt broke a 30-day streak above $3 and settled at $2.94/MMBtu, with spot around $2.80. Record US production and above-average storage have capped every heat-driven rally this summer. NOAA now puts the odds of El Niño persisting into early 2027 at 97%, which argues for a milder winter setup and keeps a lid on the strip.

The equity read-through Monday was straightforward: E&Ps and gas/LNG-levered names took the brunt of the crude selloff, refiners held up on crack spreads, and services diverged sharply on earnings.

## Screen idea

*Educational screen, not investment advice.* Screening the 22 priced watchlist names for those trading **below their 200-day moving average** produced exactly one: **HAL**, at $32.13 vs a 200-day of $33.56 (-4.3%), on TTM EV/EBITDA of **7.95x**. Every other name is above its 200-day, most by 4–14% and MPC/VLO/PSX by 31–42% — the sector has run hard and HAL is the sole laggard.

For context on valuation, the cheapest watchlist names on TTM EV/EBITDA are **DVN 4.76x**, **OXY 5.80x**, **HAL 7.95x**, **ET 9.44x**, **SLB 11.99x** and **FANG 12.69x**. DVN is the cheapest outright, but it sits 3.7% *above* its 200-day and does not pass the technical leg. HAL is the only name clearing both screens at once — roughly a third cheaper than SLB on EV/EBITDA while trading below trend.

The caveat is the obvious one: a stock can be below its 200-day and cheap because the market is right about it. Halliburton is more levered to North America land pressure pumping than SLB or BKR, and NAM completions activity has been the soft spot in every services print this cycle. The screen identifies a divergence; it does not explain it.
