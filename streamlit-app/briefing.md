## Market

Crude rose for a second straight day on Wednesday after starting the session lower. Brent January settled at about $94.61 (+3.5%) and WTI January at about $83.68 (+2.0%). That leaves Brent about $10.90 above WTI. Brent had traded near $89.90 in the morning. It then recovered on a Houthi claim of an attack on Saudi Arabia's Ras Tanura refinery (the coalition said it intercepted drones), more Iranian interdictions of ships around Hormuz, and short covering before the holiday. These settlement figures come from secondary market reports, so confirm them against exchange data.

The EIA data did not help the bulls. Crude stocks rose 1.1M bbl to 418.9M, the third straight build. Gasoline rose 1.7M bbl and distillates 0.7M. Cushing fell 534k bbl and the SPR drew 2.9M. Libya's Sharara pipeline restarted after a short halt. October PCE inflation of 3.8% y/y kept rate-hike worries in play. The oversupply story is unchanged: the IEA sees a Q4 surplus of about 1.7M b/d, and Rystad's 2027 base case is far larger.

**OPEC+:** The full ministerial meets Sunday, Nov 29, to set 2027 baselines. No production framework exists after Dec 31. The meeting will be contentious: it is the first capacity audit, Iraq is pushing for a 6.0M b/d baseline against its 4.43M quota, and roughly 2M b/d of cuts expire at year-end. No proposed quota figures have leaked.

**Natural gas:** NYMEX December expired Wednesday at about $3.27/MMBtu (-1.6%). January, now the front month, was near $3.66. EIA reported the first withdrawal of the season, 24 Bcf, above the roughly 17 Bcf WSJ consensus. Inventories are 3,858 Bcf, 80 Bcf above the five-year average. Record LNG feedgas offsets warmer early-December forecasts.

**Headlines:** There were no major US oil and gas M&A or earnings headlines. Commentary focused on dividend safety at CVX and XOM and Chevron's lithium push in the Smackover formation.
