# 2026-11-05 — Oil & Gas Briefing

## Market
Crude extended a sharp post-midterm selloff. Oilprice reported Brent down $8.94 (8.6%) to $94.66 and WTI down $8.01 (8.3%) to $88.08 on Wednesday (Nov 4), Brent's lowest since Sept 4 and far below the ~$118 seen the prior Friday. The driver was political: after Democrats won both chambers, Trump and Vance blamed the Iran war for the losses, and Trump said there were no immediate plans to strike Iran. Traders priced in a lower chance of renewed escalation. Prices bounced intraday on an unconfirmed report of US-Iran progress, which an Iranian official dismissed. Early Thursday, Tradingpedia had WTI around $86.40, near its 100-day average with RSI ~35. TD Securities still sees Brent fair value at $90–100 in a "higher-for-longer" regime. Prices quoted across sources differ by timing, so treat levels as approximate.

Inventories were supportive: EIA showed crude -865k bbl to 409.8M (a build was expected), gasoline -222k, distillates -3.6M bbl to 99.3M, refinery utilization 94.5%. Heating oil fell ~7% after DOE said it would loan 300k bbl from the Northeast Home Heating Oil Reserve.

OPEC+ held December targets steady for a third month while Hormuz disruption persists, so there's no new quota news. I could not retrieve a reliable current Henry Hub price from search; natural gas coverage showed storage builds and prices near the mid-$2s to $2.70s earlier, so verify before relying on it.

Equities followed crude lower: every priced name in the watchlist fell on Thursday, with the services group and midstream hit hardest.
