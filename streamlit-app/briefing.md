# Oil & Gas Briefing — Sunday, October 11, 2026

*Markets closed; data below reflects Friday Oct 9 closes. Educational only, not investment advice.*

## Market

Crude finished the week higher on Middle East risk and a Gulf of Mexico hurricane. Brent settled at $104.72 (+0.4% on the day, +2.4% on the week) after jumping more than 4% Thursday. WTI settled at $91.85 (+0.4%, +0.8% on the week). Prices backed off intraday highs after Trump said the US was holding "productive discussions" with Iran, though the blockade on Iranian shipments stays in force and Treasury sanctioned 17 more vessels. Houthi strikes hit Saudi airports, and attacks on ships near the Strait of Hormuz picked up. Trump also said he does not plan to order strikes on Iran before the midterms, which eased the supply scare somewhat.

Hurricane Isaias hit the US Gulf coast, with roughly 63% of Gulf of Mexico oil output shut in ahead of landfall (MUFG put about 500 kb/d at risk). Watch refinery and export-terminal restarts this week. On the demand and product side, the G7/IEA plan to release about 100 million barrels of diesel and other emergency stocks over several months capped rallies earlier in the month, and China's suspension of product exports for October keeps diesel, gasoline and jet tight in Asia. The Brent-WTI spread is wide, around $13.

Natural gas: EIA reported an 85 Bcf injection for the week ended Oct 2, taking storage to 3,500 Bcf, 68 Bcf above the five-year average but 130 Bcf below last year. Front-month gas was reported holding above about $3.20 on Oct 8 (contract not confirmed). The next EIA report is Thursday.

OPEC+: the group agreed on Oct 4 to hold November output targets steady (I could not retrieve the full article, so details and the next meeting date are unverified). The September pause after four straight monthly hikes is still the baseline.

Company headlines: nothing market-moving surfaced in searches for XOM, CVX or OXY in the last 24 hours. Chevron's Hess acquisition has closed (see note below).
